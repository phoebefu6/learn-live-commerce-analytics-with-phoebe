See it: what the camera shows

Session 2 left you 7,200 JPEGs, one per second of stream, and a scene-cut count per minute. Tonight the frames become columns. A hand landmarker says where the host's fingers are, a pose landmarker says whether she is in frame and how close, an open-vocabulary detector prompted with the words "serum bottle" says where the product is, and an OCR model reads the price card. Each of those is a per-frame answer; the minute table wants one number per minute, so the session is as much about aggregation as detection. And before any of it runs, the licence page: the most accurate detector on the shelf cannot be shipped inside a closed-source MCN dashboard without a cheque, and that decision is made tonight, not at launch.

LiveWhat these columns are worth before you build them3 min▶

A detector budget is easier to justify with the number it protects. In the Lumen Glow data, comparing minutes within the same stream so the room size and the host cancel out, the visual columns move the click-through rate on the pinned card: a minute with the product in hand runs +1.38 points of CTR, a minute of close-up runs +1.01, and a minute with the price on screen runs +0.97. Those are among the largest within-stream CTR effects in the table; only the CTA and demo segments beat them.

The same three columns do almost nothing to the click-to-order rate: product in hand +0.04 points, close-up +0.46, price overlay -0.13, none of them distinguishable from zero. The camera earns the click. What the host says earns the order. Session 4 measures that half.

The bench in session 8 turns the click effect into money. Cueing the host to have the product in hand on every CTA is worth +9.7 percent of GMV on a Friday night, as a mean over 40 seeded replays. You cannot cue what you cannot measure, and you cannot measure it without tonight's column.

Self-studyThe vocabulary tonight uses4 min▶
  • Closed-vocabulary detection - a detector trained on a fixed list of classes and blind to anything else. YOLO26's COCO weights know "person" and "bottle" and have never heard of Dew Serum.
  • Open-vocabulary detection - a detector that takes a text prompt at run time and finds whatever the prompt names. "serum bottle", "pump dispenser", the SKU name on the label. No training, one string.
  • Landmark - a named keypoint on a body part. MediaPipe's HandLandmarker returns 21 per hand, PoseLandmarker returns 33 per body, each with normalised x, y and a depth estimate.
  • Zone - a polygon on the frame that reports which detections fall inside it. supervision's PolygonZone is the standard implementation.
  • Tracker id - a stable integer attached to one object across consecutive frames, so "the same bottle" can be followed. ByteTrack is the usual algorithm.
  • OCR - optical character recognition, reading text off pixels. Florence-2's OCR_WITH_REGION task returns each string with its box.
  • Licence: AGPL-3.0 - copyleft that reaches over the network. Software that uses an AGPL component must offer its own source to users, including users who only reach it through a server. Apache-2.0 and MIT are permissive: use, modify, ship closed, keep the notice.
Part 1 · covers the four detector routes, their licences, and how each column is computed

Four ways to see the serum

9 min live

There is no single "vision model" for this job. There are four routes with different strengths and, more importantly, different licences, and a working pipeline uses three of them. Read the grid by licence first and by accuracy second, because the accuracy of a detector you cannot ship is zero.

LiveThe licence question first3 min▶

Ultralytics' own licence page draws the line. AGPL-3.0 fits research, personal and fully open-source use. The Enterprise licence is required for commercial products, closed-source internal tools, and SaaS or APIs with a YOLO backend, and its pricing is quote-based. YOLOE, the open-vocabulary model, ships inside the same package, so the same terms apply to it.

Now place the thing you are building. An MCN's stream-quality dashboard, run on the MCN's own servers for the MCN's own coaches, is a closed-source internal tool. That is on the Enterprise side of the line by the licensor's own wording. So the choice is one of three, and it is made before a single frame is processed:

  • Buy Enterprise. Get the quote, budget it, ship YOLO26 and YOLOE with a clear conscience.
  • Open-source the pipeline. AGPL is satisfied if the code that uses YOLO is offered to its users. For an internal tool that means publishing it, which some MCNs will not do and most brands will not allow.
  • Use Apache-2.0 and MIT models. MediaPipe for landmarks, Grounding DINO or OWLv2 for the SKU, Florence-2 for OCR, supervision and trackers for the glue. Tonight's build-along takes this route, and the accuracy it gives up is smaller than the licence it keeps.

Read the licence page before the benchmark table. Every detector comparison online ranks on mAP. None of them ranks on whether your legal team will sign. A model with a lower mAP that you can ship beats a model with a higher mAP that you cannot, by exactly the size of the effect you are trying to measure.

LiveHow each column is computed3 min▶

Each column is one rule applied per frame, then one aggregation per minute. The rules are deliberately simple; the sophistication is in the detectors underneath, not in the arithmetic on top.

ColumnPer-frame ruleFromPer-minute
host_on_cameraA person is present: any pose landmarks returned, or a person boxPoseLandmarkerShare of frames
close_up_ratioPerson box area over frame area. When the hip landmarks are missing, the shot is shoulders-and-up: use the box from the visible landmarksPoseLandmarkerMean
product_in_handA hand landmark lies inside, or within a margin of, a box the open-vocab detector returned for "serum bottle" or the SKU nameHandLandmarker + Grounding DINO + PolygonZoneShare of frames

scene_cutsA scene boundary, from the full-rate videoPySceneDetect, session 2Count

The one that needs care is close-up. A host who leans in until only her face and the bottle fill the frame has no hips in shot, so a naive "person box" from pose landmarks shrinks to the visible points and the ratio drops when it should rise. Treat missing hips as a signal, not as missing data: shoulders-and-up with no hips is a close-up by construction, and the ratio is computed from the shoulder-to-crown box scaled to the frame.

Self-studyThe two API signatures you will type most4 min▶

Both quoted from the fetched documentation. The Grounding DINO call is from the transformers v5.17.0 model docs; note the kwarg name, because the model card on the Hub still shows the older box_threshold while the current processor takes threshold. Signature defaults are 0.25 for both thresholds; the docs' example raises them to 0.4 and 0.3.

★ Grounding DINO, transformers v5.17.0 docs processor = AutoProcessor.from_pretrained("IDEA-Research/grounding-dino-tiny") model = AutoModelForZeroShotObjectDetection.from_pretrained("IDEA-Research/grounding-dino-tiny", device_map="auto") text_labels = [["a cat", "a remote control"]] inputs = processor(images=image, text=text_labels, return_tensors="pt").to(model.device) results = processor.post_process_grounded_object_detection( outputs, inputs.input_ids, threshold=0.4, text_threshold=0.3, target_sizes=[image.size[::-1]]) # signature defaults: threshold=0.25, text_threshold=0.25; returns scores, boxes [x0,y0,x1,y1], labels, text_labels # NOTE: the model card still shows the older kwarg name box_threshold; the v5.17 processor uses `threshold`.

MediaPipe's task options all share one shape: a base_options pointing at a model file, a running mode, and a handful of confidence thresholds. The hand landmarker's, as documented:

★ MediaPipe 1.0.1 HandLandmarkerOptions HandLandmarkerOptions(num_hands=1, min_hand_detection_confidence=0.5, min_hand_presence_confidence=0.5, min_tracking_confidence=0.5) # output per hand: handedness, hand_landmarks (21 points, normalised x,y + z), hand_world_landmarks (metres)

For a host who sells with both hands, set num_hands=2. The landmarks are normalised to the frame, so a box from the detector in pixels has to be divided by the frame width and height before the two are compared, or the landmarks multiplied up. Pick one convention and assert it in the code.

Part 2 · covers aggregation from frames to minutes, validation without ground truth, and the AI-host streams

From frames to a minute column

8 min live

A detector answers per frame. The table wants per minute. Sixty frames go in, one number comes out, and the rule that turns sixty answers into one decides whether the column means anything. Booleans become shares, ratios become means, events become counts, and a minute the detector barely saw becomes null rather than zero.

LiveThe aggregation rules3 min▶
  • Booleans become shares. A detector says true or false per frame. Sixty of those in a minute are a proportion, and the proportion is what carries information: a minute where the product was in hand for 55 frames is different from one where it was for 12. Round it to 0 or 1 only when a downstream method needs a flag, and keep the share beside it.
  • Ratios become means. Close-up ratio is already a number per frame; average it over the usable frames. A median is defensible too, but the mean is what the course table carries, so use the mean to reconcile.
  • Events become counts. Scene cuts are boundaries; a minute has an integer number of them. Session 2 built this one.
  • Thin minutes become null. If fewer than 30 of the minute's 60 frames were usable, whether because the detector crashed, the frames were black, or you sampled down for speed, write null. A share of 3 out of 4 frames is not a share, it is a coin flip with a decimal point. Session 5 skips nulls; it cannot skip a zero it does not know is fake.

The course table stores product_in_hand and price_overlay as 0 or 1 per minute, which is the share rounded at one half. Your own pipeline should keep both: the share for modelling, the flag for reconciling against the table and for the cue rules in session 8.

LiveValidating a detector without ground truth3 min▶

Nobody hands you labelled frames of your host. You make them, and it is cheaper than it sounds. Sample 200 frames at random across streams, hosts and hours; sit with a coffee and mark each one by hand for each boolean column and a rough close-up bucket. Then run the detector on the same 200 and report precision and recall per column, not one accuracy number: a detector that never says "in hand" has 90 percent accuracy on a stream where the product is rarely held, and is useless.

Keep the 200 labels in the repository. They are the regression test for every future change of model, threshold or prompt, and they are the answer when someone asks how much to trust a column. A detector whose recall on price cards is low will understate the price-overlay effect, and knowing that before session 6 is worth more than a better model after it.

Label the disagreements twice. Frames where two of you disagree on "in hand" are the frames the rule is unclear on, not the frames the detector is wrong on. Write the rule down (touching counts, hovering does not, the margin is one hand-width) and re-label. The detector's zone margin is that rule in pixels.

LiveWhat the AI-host streams look like on camera2 min▶

Two of the six Lumen Glow hosts are AI avatars, and the camera columns spot them before anyone reads the host name. Their scene cuts sit at 1 to 3 per minute against 2 to 7 for the human hosts, because an avatar has no operator switching to the product camera. Their speech rate holds at a steady 158 words a minute with almost no variance, where a human host's rate moves with the segment and collapses in dead air. The columns are honest about it: the avatars are consistent, and consistency is not what sells.

On TikTok Shop this is a compliance question before it is an analytics question. The Requirements for High-Quality Videos and LIVEs, effective 2026-09-17 in the US, say "Do not use non-real-time verbal interaction such as AI-generated voices, audio recordings, or radio" and "Do not use animated figures or content that covers more than 50% of the screen." A fully AI-voiced avatar host is not permitted on a promotional LIVE there; the AI streams in the data show what the trade would be on a platform that allows it. Leader session 4 covers the rule; tonight's close_up_ratio is, incidentally, the measurement of "more than 50% of the screen".

Self-studyWhat a cloud VLM labeller costs against a local detector3 min▶

There is a fourth way to get columns from frames: send the video to a vision-language model and ask it. Gemini 3.8 Flash reads video at about 100 tokens per second at low resolution and is priced at $0.75 per 1M input tokens under promotional pricing that runs through 2026-12-31, which works out to about $0.006 per minute of video. Session 4 uses it as a segment labeller, where a model that understands "she is now doing the price pitch" earns its keep.

For tonight's columns it is the wrong tool for three reasons. A VLM returns a sentence, not a box, so "in hand" becomes a judgement you cannot audit against a zone margin. Its answer per frame is not stable across runs, so the 200-frame validation drifts. And the price is per minute for as long as you keep running it, where a local Apache-2.0 landmarker costs the electricity. Use the VLM for what needs language, the detector for what needs geometry, and keep the receipts for both.

Build-along

Five columns from frames

★ 20 min · everyone builds

Twenty minutes, six steps, nothing AGPL. You run landmarkers and an open-vocabulary detector on the 1 fps frames from session 2, apply the zone rule, aggregate to the minute, and reconcile against the columns the course table already carries. On the course data the columns are precomputed, so the reconcile step passes on the first run; on your own recording the same code overwrites them with what your camera actually showed.

Install the permissive stack. Pin the versions; the trackers move between packages and the kwarg names change between releases, and a pin is the only thing that makes next month's run reproduce tonight's.

★ Versions read 2026-09-24 pip install "mediapipe==1.0.1" "supervision==0.30.5" "trackers==2.6.0" \ "transformers==5.17.0" "torch" "pillow" "pandas" "numpy" # Grounding DINO 1.0 weights: IDEA-Research/grounding-dino-tiny (Apache-2.0) # Florence-2 for OCR: microsoft/Florence-2-large (MIT), needs trust_remote_code=True # MediaPipe .task files: hand_landmarker.task, pose_landmarker.task from the MediaPipe model pages

Landmarks on every frame. Both landmarkers in VIDEO running mode, fed the 1 fps frames in order with a timestamp in milliseconds, so the tracker inside each task can use the previous frame. Frame n is second n, so the timestamp is n times 1000.

★ MediaPipe 1.0.1, hand and pose, VIDEO mode import mediapipe as mp from mediapipe.tasks.python import vision from pathlib import Path import pandas as pd RM = vision.RunningMode.VIDEO hand_opts = vision.HandLandmarkerOptions( base_options=mp.tasks.BaseOptions(model_asset_path="models/hand_landmarker.task"), running_mode=RM, num_hands=2, min_hand_detection_confidence=0.5, min_hand_presence_confidence=0.5, min_tracking_confidence=0.5) pose_opts = vision.PoseLandmarkerOptions( base_options=mp.tasks.BaseOptions(model_asset_path="models/pose_landmarker.task"), running_mode=RM, num_poses=1, output_segmentation_masks=False) rows = [] frames = sorted(Path("frames").glob("*.jpg")) # 000001.jpg is second 1 with vision.HandLandmarker.create_from_options(hand_opts) as hands, \ vision.PoseLandmarker.create_from_options(pose_opts) as pose: for f in frames: n = int(f.stem) img = mp.Image.create_from_file(str(f)) h = hands.detect_for_video(img, n * 1000) p = pose.detect_for_video(img, n * 1000) lm = p.pose_landmarks[0] if p.pose_landmarks else None on_cam = lm is not None # close-up: box of visible landmarks over the frame; no hips visible = shoulders-and-up shot if on_cam: vis = [q for q in lm if q.visibility > 0.5] xs, ys = [q.x for q in vis], [q.y for q in vis] hips_visible = lm[23].visibility > 0.5 or lm[24].visibility > 0.5 box = (max(xs) - min(xs)) * (max(ys) - min(ys)) # normalised, so already / frame area close = box if hips_visible else max(box, 0.5) # shoulders-and-up is a close-up by construction else: close = None hand_pts = [(q.x, q.y) for hl in h.hand_landmarks for q in hl] rows.append({"sec": n, "host_on_camera": on_cam, "close_up_ratio": close, "hand_pts": hand_pts}) fr = pd.DataFrame(rows)

Find the serum by name. Grounding DINO 1.0 with the SKU as the prompt. The processor's post-processing takes threshold in transformers 5.17; the model card still says box_threshold, and passing that name errors. Take the highest-scoring box per frame; the host holds one bottle at a time.

★ Grounding DINO 1.0, one prompt, one box per frame import torch from PIL import Image from transformers import AutoProcessor, AutoModelForZeroShotObjectDetection processor = AutoProcessor.from_pretrained("IDEA-Research/grounding-dino-tiny") model = AutoModelForZeroShotObjectDetection.from_pretrained("IDEA-Research/grounding-dino-tiny", device_map="auto") text_labels = [["serum bottle"]] # or the SKU name printed on the label boxes = {} for f in frames: image = Image.open(f).convert("RGB") inputs = processor(images=image, text=text_labels, return_tensors="pt").to(model.device) with torch.no_grad(): outputs = model(**inputs) res = processor.post_process_grounded_object_detection( outputs, inputs.input_ids, threshold=0.4, text_threshold=0.3, target_sizes=[image.size[::-1]])[0] if len(res["boxes"]): i = int(res["scores"].argmax()) boxes[int(f.stem)] = (res["boxes"][i].tolist(), image.size) # [x0,y0,x1,y1] in pixels, (W,H)

The hand-near-product rule as a zone. Expand the detected box by a margin and make it a PolygonZone. Turn each hand landmark into a tiny detection and ask the zone whether it is inside. Landmarks are normalised and the box is in pixels, so scale the landmarks up; assert the convention once.

★ supervision 0.30.5 PolygonZone around the product box import numpy as np import supervision as sv MARGIN = 0.15 # of box width; "one hand-width" in your labelling rule def in_hand(sec): if sec not in boxes: return False (x0, y0, x1, y1), (W, H) = boxes[sec] m = MARGIN * (x1 - x0) zone = sv.PolygonZone(polygon=np.array([[x0-m, y0-m], [x1+m, y0-m], [x1+m, y1+m], [x0-m, y1+m]]).astype(int), triggering_anchors=(sv.Position.CENTER,)) pts = fr.loc[fr.sec == sec, "hand_pts"].iloc[0] if not pts: return False assert all(0 <= x <= 1 and 0 <= y <= 1 for x, y in pts), "landmarks must be normalised" xyxy = np.array([[x*W - 1, y*H - 1, x*W + 1, y*H + 1] for x, y in pts]) # 2 px squares at each landmark return bool(zone.trigger(sv.Detections(xyxy=xyxy)).any()) fr["product_in_hand"] = fr.sec.map(in_hand)

Aggregate to the minute. Shares for booleans, mean for the ratio, count for cuts, and null where fewer than 30 frames were usable. Price overlay comes from Florence-2's OCR_WITH_REGION run the same way as step 3; the per-frame rule is "a currency symbol followed by digits appeared anywhere", and it is merged in here as a boolean column called price_seen.

★ pandas: sixty answers to one row import re fr["t"] = (fr.sec - 1) // 60 + 1 # minute 1 is seconds 1..60 fr["usable"] = fr.host_on_camera.notna() # the detector ran and returned # price_seen: from Florence-2 OCR_WITH_REGION per frame, e.g. bool(re.search(r"[$£€]\s?\d", text)) g = fr[fr.usable].groupby("t") minute = pd.DataFrame({ "usable_frames": g.size(), "product_in_hand_share": g.product_in_hand.mean(), "host_on_camera": g.host_on_camera.mean(), "close_up_ratio": g.close_up_ratio.mean(), "price_overlay_share": g.price_seen.mean(), }).reindex(range(1, 121)) minute.loc[minute.usable_frames.fillna(0) < 30, minute.columns.drop("usable_frames")] = None minute["scene_cuts"] = scene_cuts # session 2's per-minute count minute["product_in_hand"] = (minute.product_in_hand_share >= 0.5).astype("Int64") minute["price_overlay"] = (minute.price_overlay_share >= 0.5).astype("Int64") minute.round(2).head()

Reconcile against the course table, then overwrite with your own. The 7,200-row table already carries product_in_hand, close_up_ratio, price_overlay and scene_cuts for every row. On the course data your pipeline should reproduce them; on your own recording it replaces them, and host_on_camera joins the table as a new column either way.

★ Same key, same columns, your numbers m = pd.read_csv("data/streams.csv") cols = ["product_in_hand", "close_up_ratio", "price_overlay", "scene_cuts"] assert m[cols].notna().all().all() and len(m) == 7200 # precomputed for the course data mine = minute.reset_index().rename(columns={"index": "t"}).assign(stream_id=STREAM_ID) m = m.merge(mine[["stream_id", "t", "host_on_camera", "usable_frames"] + cols], on=["stream_id", "t"], how="left", suffixes=("", "_mine")) for c in cols: # your detector wins where it ran m[c] = m[f"{c}_mine"].where(m[f"{c}_mine"].notna(), m[c]) m = m.drop(columns=[f"{c}_mine" for c in cols]) m.to_csv("data/derived/minute_with_vision.csv", index=False)

Check yourself

Three questions

An MCN wants to ship a stream-quality dashboard for its own coaches. It runs on the MCN's servers and the code is not published. Which detector stack can it ship without buying a licence?

The Ultralytics licence page names closed-source internal tools as a case that requires the Enterprise licence, and YOLOE ships inside the same package. The permissive stack can run closed, on a server, without a cheque; the price is a little accuracy and a little more glue code.

The detector says true or false per frame. Why does the minute table carry product_in_hand as a share of frames rather than a single true or false?

Aggregation should lose as little as possible. The share is the natural statistic of sixty booleans; the course table's 0/1 flag is the share rounded at one half, and your own pipeline should keep both, along with usable_frames as the denominator.

Within a stream, what do the camera columns move, and what do they leave alone?

The within-stream CTOR effects for the same three columns are +0.04, +0.46 and -0.13 points, none distinguishable from zero. Session 4's speech columns are where CTOR moves. That split is why session 8's cue "product in hand on every CTA" works: it pairs the camera's click with the words' order.
